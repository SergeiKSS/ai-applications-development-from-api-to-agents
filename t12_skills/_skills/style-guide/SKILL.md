# Style Guide Skill

Rewrites or reviews text so it matches the brand's voice, tone, and formatting rules.

## File Map

- `SKILL.md` (this file) — core principles and workflow.
- `REFERENCE.md` — type-specific rules (emails, blog posts, social media, support content) and
  grammar/mechanics rules. Read on demand once you know the content type.
- `EXAMPLES.md` — before/after rewrite examples per content type. Read on demand to see the rules
  applied in practice.

## Workflow

1. Identify the content type of the request (email, blog post, social media post, support reply,
   or general text).
2. Apply the Core Principles and Instant Rules below.
3. Open `REFERENCE.md` and read the section for that content type for detailed rules.
4. Open `EXAMPLES.md` and read the matching section to see concrete before/after rewrites.
5. Return the polished text, followed by a short **Changes:** line summarizing what was adjusted
   and why.

## Core Principles

- **Voice:** Confident, helpful, and human — write like a knowledgeable colleague, not a legal
  disclaimer.
- **Tone:** Warm and plain-spoken; adjust formality to the content type (more casual on social,
  more precise in support content) but never stiff or robotic.
- **Length:** Say it in as few words as possible. Cut filler, hedging, and redundant qualifiers.
- **Person:** Address the reader as "you"; speak for the company as "we". Avoid third-person
  ("the user", "the company") and passive constructions that hide who is acting.

## Instant Rules

Quick checklist to apply to every rewrite:

- Replace jargon and corporate-speak with plain English (see `REFERENCE.md` → Grammar & Mechanics).
- Prefer active voice over passive voice.
- Use contractions (`we're`, `you'll`) to sound natural.
- Spell out numbers one through nine; use digits for 10 and above (except in data/stats contexts).
- Use the Oxford comma.
- Avoid exclamation marks outside of casual social media copy; keep punctuation calm and direct.
